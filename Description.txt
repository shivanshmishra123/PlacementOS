I am planning to build a project called placementOS. What it will do is that i am myself a placement coordinator and I have personally felt this need :

Problem: Placement coordinators manually handle job descriptions, eligibility criteria, spreadsheets, student communications, scheduling, and candidate documentation.

PlacementOS is a platform that automates the operational lifecycle of campus recruitment drives, from receiving a company's Job Description to managing student eligibility, documents, communications, interviews, and final results.

A company sends its Job Description (JD) and eligibility criteria to the placement cell. The coordinators must then:
Read the JD and manually extract the company details, job role, CTC, eligibility criteria, important dates, and selection process.
Check hundreds of students against the eligibility criteria.
Identify missing or incorrectly formatted documents.
Prepare an eligible-student list or registration form.
Compose and distribute announcements.
Track registrations and document submissions.
Coordinate aptitude tests, interviews, venues, and changes.
Inform students of updates and record results.

Much of this involves manual checking across emails, PDFs, Google Sheets, forms, and messages.

You upload the company's PDF or select the email containing the JD.
PlacementOS then performs the following workflow.
The JD agent interprets the document.
It extracts all relevant fields into structured data, identifies ambiguity, and shows a source-backed summary for review.
The eligibility engine checks student records.
Instead of an LLM guessing who is eligible, deterministic rules evaluate branch, CGPA, graduation year, and backlog status.
The document agent reviews required files.
It identifies missing resumes, invalid filenames, unreadable PDFs, and incorrect document formats.
The communication agent prepares announcements.
It generates relevant emails for approved audiences, with registration details and deadline information.
The coordinator approves the proposed actions.
PlacementOS pauses until an authorized coordinator verifies the drive details, recipient lists, and outgoing communications.
The system executes and monitors the drive.
It sends approved communications, tracks submissions, and follows up on missing documents or schedule changes.
That makes LangGraph useful: a workflow can pause for approval, persist its state, and resume after the coordinator responds.


I would not create one massive AI agent with access to the entire database, Gmail, Google Sheets, and Calendar. That would be difficult to test, expensive, and potentially unsafe.
Instead, use LangGraph as the orchestration layer, with specialized LangChain agents and deterministic backend services.

5. Detailed responsibilities of every agent
Agent 1 — JD Intelligence Agent
Purpose: Convert unstructured recruitment requirements into structured, verifiable data.
Input: PDF, DOCX, or email containing the job description.

Agent 2 — Eligibility and Policy Engine
This is the most sensitive module.

I would deliberately implement it as a deterministic rules engine, supported by an AI agent that interprets requirements and diagnoses exceptions.

For example, imagine these fictional records:
Student	Branch	CGPA	Active backlog	Result
A	CSE	8.2	No	Eligible
B	ECE	8.8	No	Ineligible
C	IT	7.2	No	Ineligible
D	CSE	8.0	Yes	Ineligible
E	IT	7.9	Unknown	Needs review

The engine should evaluate a transparent logical expression:
\[
E(s) = B(s) \land C(s) \land G(s) \land N(s)
\]

Where:
- \(B(s)\): Student belongs to an allowed branch.
- \(C(s)\): Student satisfies the CGPA threshold.
- \(G(s)\): Graduation year matches.
- \(N(s)\): No active backlog, as required.
A student is eligible only when all required conditions are verified as true.

When required information is missing, the result should be Needs Review, not automatically eligible or ineligible.

Agent 4 — Communication and Outreach Agent
Placement coordinators need to send multiple kinds of messages.
Examples include drive announcements, missing-document reminders, registration deadline warnings, interview schedules, and updates when interview timings change.
The agent receives structured drive information and the intended recipient audience.
It drafts a communication, verifies that required fields are present, and sends it to an approval queue.
It should never decide independently to send sensitive or bulk communications without authorization.

Agent 5 — Exception Resolution Agent
Advanced feature
This is what could make PlacementOS substantially more interesting than a conventional workflow automation tool.
Real placement operations rarely follow a perfectly predictable sequence.
Imagine these situations:
A company revises the minimum CGPA from 7.5 to 8.0.
A student corrects an outdated academic record.
An approved announcement contains a deadline that subsequently changes.
The email provider fails while delivering notifications.
A company updates its eligible branches halfway through registration.
Your Exception Resolution Agent would identify the type of problem, retrieve the relevant records, propose a recovery plan, and request approval before taking consequential actions.
For the CGPA change, it could propose to version the eligibility policy, recompute affected students, identify previously notified candidates, and prepare a correction announcement.
The actual changes would be executed by controlled backend services.
This provides a genuinely interesting engineering challenge: event-driven workflows that adapt to changing information without silently corrupting their state.
